02 — Who Makes the Decision
Decision Maker Personas
EVS contracts are committee decisions — every stakeholder requires a distinct value message
ICP / Psychographic
CF

Chief Financial Officer

Age 45–58 · MBA/CPA · 5+ yrs in role · Reports to CEO · Controls budget sign-off

Core Motivations

  • Reduce labor overhead and benefits cost
  • Convert fixed EVS cost to variable
  • Improve operating margin
  • Avoid CMS penalty exposure from HAIs
  • Board-level optics on "right-sizing"

Primary Fears

  • Surprise cost escalations post-contract
  • Vendor lock-in with deteriorating performance
  • Staff morale / union friction at transition
  • Reputational damage from high-profile incident
  • Loss of control over a clinical-adjacent function
"We need to get EVS off the payroll, but the last company we used left us with worse scores and a staffing mess."

What Wins Them Over

ROI models with cost-per-bed benchmarks. Peer hospital comparisons. Guaranteed KPI contracts with penalty clauses. Case studies showing margin improvement. The CFO needs to defend the decision to the board — give them the data.

CO

COO / VP of Operations

Age 42–55 · MHA or clinical background · Day-to-day operator · Often the internal champion

Core Motivations

  • Smoother day-to-day hospital operations
  • Reduce admin burden of managing EVS staff
  • Improve patient throughput and room turnover
  • Better staffing reliability and coverage
  • Defend HCAHPS and Joint Commission scores

Primary Fears

  • Disruption and chaos during transition period
  • Clinical staff complaints about cleanliness
  • Vendor unresponsiveness on-site
  • Losing institutional knowledge of current EVS team
  • Finger-pointing when patient incidents arise
"I don't care who cleans the rooms — I care that rooms are ready when surgery needs them and that I'm not fielding complaints at 10pm."

What Wins Them Over

Detailed transition playbooks. On-site management presence commitments. Real-time dashboards (XRO system). References from peer hospital COOs. Dedicated named account manager with direct phone number.

EV

Director of Environmental Services

Age 38–52 · Often internal hire · High-influence recommender OR silent veto

Core Motivations

  • Retain or secure their own role post-transition
  • Access to better training programs (AORN Skills Fair)
  • Modern equipment and protocols (XRO-style)
  • Earn respect from clinical peers
  • Reduce vacancy and overtime pain

Primary Fears

  • Being made redundant after outsourcing
  • Frontline staff turnover or cultural clash
  • Vendor lowballing wages, causing attrition
  • Being blamed for a failed transition
"I've seen companies come in and gut the team we built. I need to know my people will be treated right — and that I still have a seat at the table."

What Wins Them Over

Role-retention guarantees. Pay-parity pledges in writing. Transparent staffing model demos (XanStaff). Technology demonstrations. Peer EVS director reference calls from existing Xanitos sites.

CN

Chief Nursing Officer

Age 48–60 · Clinical champion · Influential veto voice · Patient outcomes owner

Core Motivations

  • Safe patient environment, zero HAIs
  • Faster room turnaround times for nursing workflow
  • EVS responsiveness to nursing requests
  • Higher patient satisfaction scores (HCAHPS)

Primary Fears

  • EVS contractors who don't understand clinical culture
  • Inconsistent cleaning quality on night/weekend shifts
  • Communication breakdowns between EVS and nursing
  • C. diff or MRSA outbreaks linked to EVS lapses
"My nurses need EVS to be an extension of the care team, not a vendor who clocks out at 5."

What Wins Them Over

Clinical partnership language throughout the proposal. AORN certification credentials for EVS staff. HAI data from peer facilities. Direct testimonials from charge nurses at existing Xanitos reference sites.

IP

Infection Prevention Officer

Age 40–58 · CIC-certified · Technical validator · Can kill or accelerate a deal

Core Motivations

  • Measurable HAI reduction, documented outcomes
  • CDC and Joint Commission compliance confidence
  • Validated cleaning protocols for isolation rooms
  • Access to UV-C and electrostatic technology

Primary Fears

  • Vendor cutting corners on terminal cleans
  • Inadequate outbreak response protocols
  • Staff not trained in isolation precautions
  • Compliance gaps flagged during surveys
"Show me your C. diff terminal cleaning SOP and your fluorescent marker audit scores."

What Wins Them Over

Published clinical outcomes data. XRO methodology documentation. ATP/fluorescent marker audit results from active sites. Willingness to co-develop infection prevention protocols alongside the hospital's IP team.

04 — When They Buy
Contract Trigger Events
Xanitos wins when specific organizational events create urgency — identify these in prospect research
ICP / Trigger Map
01

Joint Commission or CMS Survey Failure

Cleanliness citations create immediate urgency. Often produces a 60–90 day procurement fast-track. The board gets involved. This is the highest-urgency trigger — status quo becomes politically untenable overnight.

High urgency
02

HAI Outbreak or C. diff Spike

Infection events generate board-level pressure, press coverage risk, and legal exposure. At $24,205 per C. diff case, a single outbreak makes the cost of outsourcing trivial. Creates emotional as well as financial urgency.

High urgency
03

EVS Staffing Crisis (Vacancies >25%)

Inability to recruit and retain in-house EVS staff is the #1 operational driver of outsourcing decisions. When overtime costs spiral and rooms aren't being turned, CFOs look for the exit. Xanitos reduced one client's vacancies 75% and OT 70%.

Medium urgency
04

Incumbent Contract Renewal or Dissatisfaction

Most bids come from hospitals already outsourced to a competitor (Crothall, ABM, HHS) whose performance has slipped. The 12-month window before contract expiry is the primary prospecting sweet spot.

Medium urgency
05

Hospital Merger or System Acquisition

New system leadership standardizes vendors across the portfolio — creating large multi-site RFP opportunities. A single win at the system level can unlock 3–8 hospitals simultaneously. Watch for M&A activity in target markets.

Strategic
06

Operating Margin Pressure or Budget Reset

CFO-led cost transformation initiatives put all labor-heavy departments under outsourcing review. EVS is the largest controllable labor line in facility services — it's always on the list when margins compress.

A peer phone call beats any proposal document. Current Xanitos clients making reference calls is the fastest close path in the playbook.

Patented systems no competitor can replicate. Tie each feature directly to the prospect's specific pain metrics.

PROSPECTING WINDOW

Monitor competitor contract award dates via trade databases. An unhappy client 14 months into a 3-year deal is actively exploring options even if their contract won't expire for 22 months.

PRICING LEVERAGE

Labor pass-through pricing removes margin risk from Xanitos but requires crystal-clear SOW. Upsell levers include ambulatory cleaning, patient transport, and laundry — typically added in contract years 2–4.

RENEWAL STRATEGY

Renewal risk spikes when HCAHPS scores plateau and the CFO starts comparing costs again. Protect the account by delivering visible technology upgrades and providing quarterly performance reviews that tie back to financial outcomes.
